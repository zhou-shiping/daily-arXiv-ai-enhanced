Motivation: The conjecture by Benguria, Levitin, and Parnovski (2009) asserted that among centrally symmetric convex bodies of fixed volume, the Euclidean ball maximizes the distance from the zero set of the Fourier transform of its characteristic function to the origin. The author seeks to test and challenge this long-standing conjecture.

Method: The study analyzes the zero set of the Fourier transform of the characteristic function of centrally symmetric convex bodies with fixed volume, and constructs examples (regular centrally symmetric polygons, especially the dodecagon) to compare against the disk, and examines higher dimensions.

Result: The conjecture is disproved in all dimensions greater than one. In the plane, every regular centrally symmetric polygon with at least twelve sides outperforms the disk by a tiny margin, with the regular dodecagon being best among them. In dimensions three and higher, no maximizer exists.

Conclusion: The Benguria–Levitin–Parnovski conjecture is false: the disk is not optimal, and in dimensions three and above no maximizer exists at all.

Abstract: Given a centrally symmetric convex body, consider the zero set of the Fourier transform of its characteristic function. We study how large the distance from this set to the origin can be when the volume of the body is fixed. A 2009 conjecture of Benguria, Levitin, and Parnovski asserts that the maximum is attained by a Euclidean ball. We disprove it in all dimensions greater than one. In the plane, every regular centrally symmetric polygon with at least twelve sides outperforms the disk, albeit by a tiny margin, and the regular dodecagon is best among them. In dimensions three and higher, no maximiser exists.
