Motivation: Rare-earth transition-metal borides are critical for permanent-magnet design, yet the manganese-rich regions of these compositional phase spaces remain largely unexplored. Method: The authors develop a multi-stage machine-learning-assisted discovery framework: (1) generate over one million hypothetical structures from known database structures, (2) filter candidates using graph neural networks to predict material stability, (3) relax structures using machine-learning interatomic potentials, and (4) validate results with first-principles density functional theory calculations. These findings indicate that targeted transition-metal substitution within a one-dimensional boride family can recover transition-metal magnetism, offering a physically interpretable route for designing new magnetic rare-earth transition-metal borides.

Method: The authors prove two implications: (Q_n) implies (P_n), and (P_n) implies (Q_{n+1}), creating a bootstrap argument that propagates both results across all dimensions.

Abstract: For integers $n\ge 1$, consider assertions:
