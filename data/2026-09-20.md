### [93] [Learning the Emergent Bulk Geometry of the Three Dimensional Ising Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20210)
*Ritam Basu*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: Machine learning reconstructs a bulk AdS4 geometry from 3D Ising model data, finding that no single classical metric can fit both boundary operators simultaneously.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Testing whether a single emergent bulk geometry (holographic dual) can describe a strongly coupled theory with O(1) central charge like the 3D Ising model.

Method: Train a differentiable probe-field solver on an asymptotically AdS4 background to reproduce Monte Carlo two-point functions of the σ and ε operators at criticality, fitting each operator channel separately.

Result: A single metric cannot serve both operators. Per-channel fitting improves its own residual 3-5x but degrades the other by up to two orders of magnitude; this tension persists across momentum windows and lattice sizes.

Conclusion: The bulk geometry of a theory with O(1) central charge, such as the 3D Ising model, is not captured by a single classical metric.

Abstract: We use Monte Carlo data for the three-dimensional Ising model at the critical temperature to predict an emergent radial geometry of the bulk using machine learning. Specifically, we employ a differentiable solver for a probe field on an asymptotically $\mathrm{AdS}_4$ background, trained to reproduce the measured momentum-space two-point functions of the boundary operators ($σ$ and $ε$). We find that a single metric cannot simultaneously serve both operators. Fitting each channel separately improves its own residual by a factor of three to five, but degrades the other by up to two orders of magnitude. We quantify this tension between the two operators and show that it survives changes to the momentum window and the lattice size. Our results numerically demonstrate that the bulk geometry of a theory with an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ central charge, such as the 3D Ising model, is not captured by a single classical metric.

### [94] [Effects of radial conductivity variation on the Ponomarenko dynamo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19187)
*Shipra Verma,Kannabiran Seshasayanan*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper examines how radial conductivity variation affects the Ponomarenko dynamo, showing that the critical magnetic Reynolds number and growth rate depend sensitively on the conductivity profile near the velocity discontinuity.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The Ponomarenko dynamo is a classic kinematic dynamo model, but the effect of spatially varying conductivity on its onset and growth is not well understood. This work aims to clarify how conductivity variations modify dynamo thresholds and growth rates.

Method: The authors solve the kinematic dynamo problem with monotonically increasing/decreasing and sinusoidally varying radial conductivity profiles, analyzing the critical magnetic Reynolds number Rm_c and deriving an asymptotic growth-rate expression for large Rm.

Result: Rm_c depends strongly on the form of conductivity variation near the velocity discontinuity. Monotonic profiles map to the conductivity-jump problem in the sharp-variation limit, while sinusoidal profiles with large variations/wavenumbers increase Rm_c due to effective diffusivity. Far from threshold, the growth rate depends only on the local conductivity gradient; opposite signs of shear and conductivity gradient enhance the growth rate. The large-Rm correction is proportional to the local conductivity gradient times Rm^{-1/2}.

Conclusion: Conductivity variation near the shear discontinuity controls dynamo onset and growth, with the leading correction depending only on the local gradient there. Similar dependencies hold for smooth velocity profiles and different magnetic boundary conditions.

Abstract: We study the effects of conductivity variation on the Ponomarenko dynamo. Taking monotonically increasing/decreasing and sinusoidally varying radial profiles, we study the kinematic dynamo problem. The threshold of the dynamo, given by the critical magnetic Reynolds number Rm$_c$, is found to strongly depend on the form of conductivity variation near the discontinuity of the velocity field where the shear is dominant. For monotonically varying profiles in the limit of sharp variation, this threshold maps to the problem of the dynamo with a jump in conductivity, while for the sinusoidal profile with large variations/wavenumbers, the threshold increases due to effective diffusivity arising from the rapid changes in conductivity. Far from the threshold, it is found that the growth rate depends only on the local gradient of the conductivity near the discontinuity, with the opposite sign of shear and conductivity gradient leading to a larger growth rate, thereby aiding the dynamo instability in regenerating B$_φ$ from B$_r$. We calculate the expression for the growth rate in the limit of large Rm$(\gg 1)$ and find that the conductivity variation leads to a modification proportional to the local gradient of conductivity times Rm$^{-1/2}$. This correction is found to depend only on the local gradient of the conductivity at the discontinuity. Similar dependencies are found for realistic, smooth velocity profiles and for different magnetic boundary conditions.

</details>
